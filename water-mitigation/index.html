Carpet Still Soaked and Walls Wet to the Touch?

If the leak has been fixed but the carpet squishes, the baseboards feel cold and damp, or the wall seems wetter than it looks, the job is not finished. Moisture is still inside the materials. The trade term for stopping that damage is water mitigation, and it is the step between the leak and the repair.

This is an emergency service because the first day matters most. Our line is open 24/7. While you wait for a crew, shut off the water at the main valve if the source is still running, move furniture and electronics off wet floors, and turn off power to wet areas only if you can do it safely from a dry spot.

What Water Mitigation Means

Water mitigation is the work done in the first 24 to 72 hours after a loss to limit how far the water spreads and how long materials stay wet. It includes stopping or isolating the source, removing standing water, setting up drying equipment and mapping moisture so the crew knows what is wet and what is dry.

It is different from repair. Mitigation protects the structure and the clock. Water damage repair rebuilds what could not be saved. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why mitigation comes first.

Drying goals and the practice of recording moisture readings are described in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which is why readings, and not how a room feels, decide when drying is finished. What to do while equipment runs: keep doors and windows closed in the work area and leave the equipment powered. What not to do: do not move or switch off air movers, since that can leave pockets wet.

Structural Drying

Structural drying is the core of mitigation. Wet drywall, framing, subfloors and flooring hold water far longer than the surface suggests, so the goal is to bring each material back to a normal moisture reading and prove it with a meter. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so the water has somewhere to go.

Crews take readings on day one and again each visit, adjusting equipment until the numbers match unaffected parts of the home. Thermal imaging helps find wet areas behind walls, and the log of readings becomes a record you can share with your insurer.

Beverly Hills Properties and the Drying Clock

Large homes in Trousdale Estates and the Flats often have long runs of hardwood, stone and plaster that need controlled drying so they do not crack or cup. Condos along Wilshire Boulevard add another wrinkle, since drying equipment has to be set up without disturbing neighbors and with building management's approval.

Water can also travel. A leak above a ceiling in a multistory home can soak insulation, run down a wall cavity and show up two rooms away. That is why mitigation starts with moisture mapping and not with the stain you can see.

What Affects the Cost of Water Mitigation

Mitigation is priced by how much equipment is needed and how long it runs. These five factors set most of the cost.

Water category

Gray and black water need removal and cleaning steps added to drying.

Affected area

More wet square footage needs more air movers and dehumidifiers.

Materials involved

Hardwood, plaster and stone dry slower than carpet and basic drywall.

Drying days and equipment

Equipment counts and days on site are the largest driver of mitigation cost.

Rebuild scope

Mitigation is priced apart from repairs, so you can see each piece.
