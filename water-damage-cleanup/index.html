The First 48 Hours After Water Damage

If water has soaked your floors, contents or walls, treat cleanup as urgent. Our line is open 24/7, and the first 48 hours decide how much can be saved. While you wait for a crew, stop the source and shut off the main water valve, lift valuables and paper goods off wet surfaces, open windows if the weather allows, and keep people and pets out of any water that might be contaminated.

Water damage cleanup is what happens once the source is stopped. Crews remove wet debris and materials that cannot be saved, sort what can stay, then clean, sanitize and deodorize the surfaces that remain. Drying the structure is a separate job, handled under water mitigation, and the two usually run side by side.

When cleanup cannot wait

Some situations should be handled right away, since waiting makes the cleanup harder.

  • ✓Water that came from a toilet, sewer line, washing machine or the outdoors
  • ✓Carpet and pad that have been wet for more than a day
  • ✓A musty smell that appeared after the leak
  • ✓Wet drywall, insulation or cabinet bases that feel soft
  • ✓Stored boxes, books or fabric sitting in or near the water

What Gets Removed and What Can Be Saved

Porous materials soak up water and anything the water carried, so they are the first to go when the water was dirty. Hard surfaces can usually be cleaned and kept. The decision depends on the water category, how long the material stayed wet and what it is made of.

Cleaning and Sanitizing After Gray or Black Water

When the water came from a toilet, sewer, washing machine or floodwater, cleanup is a hygiene job as well as a drying job. Crews wear protective gear, bag and remove materials that cannot be cleaned, wash hard surfaces and apply treatment to what remains. The EPA advises calling a professional with experience cleaning buildings damaged by contaminated water.

That is not a job for a bucket and a mop. Contaminated water carries material that spreads easily, and cleaning without removing the wet porous layer underneath leaves the problem in place. If you are unsure about the water source, treat it as contaminated until a crew has looked.

Odors, Fine Contents and Honest Limits

A musty smell after water damage usually means something is still wet, so deodorizing without drying only covers the problem. Crews find the damp material, remove or dry it, clean the surfaces and then address the odor. If the smell returns, moisture is usually still present.

Fine art, antiques, custom rugs and family photographs need different care. A restoration crew dries and cleans the structure, but irreplaceable contents belong with a specialist. If your home holds items like these, move them out of the water yourself if it is safe, photograph them for your insurer and ask a conservator or your insurer for guidance.

According to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, water is sorted into three categories, and the category decides how much has to be removed and how surfaces are cleaned. What to do first: separate wet contents from dry ones, keep children and pets out and wait for the scope before throwing anything away. What not to do: do not use household bleach and ammonia products together.

A wet kitchen in the Flats and a flooded bathroom in a Wilshire corridor condo are very different cleanups. What Affects the Cost of Water Damage Cleanup

Cleanup is priced by how much has to be removed and how dirty the water was. These five factors move the number most.

Water category

Gray and black water add removal, protective gear and treatment steps.

Affected area

More rooms and more wet square footage mean more labor and disposal.

Materials involved

Porous materials like carpet and drywall come out more often than hard surfaces.

Drying days and equipment

Cleanup and drying run together, so the equipment schedule affects the total.

Rebuild scope

What replaces the removed materials is priced separately, afterward.
