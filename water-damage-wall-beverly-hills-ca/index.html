Water Damage on a Wall in Beverly Hills, CA: What to Do

A wet wall hides most of its problem inside the cavity. Here is how to judge the damage, dry it and decide what to replace.

Why a Wet Wall Is Bigger Than It Looks

A wall is a hollow box. Behind the paint are drywall or plaster, framing, insulation and sometimes pipes and wiring. When water gets in, it wicks upward through the drywall, runs down inside the cavity and pools at the bottom plate.

What shows on the surface is only part of the loss. A stain the size of a hand can sit on a wall that is wet across several feet. Treat the stain as a clue and measure the rest.

What Drywall and Plaster Do When Wet

Gypsum drywall absorbs water readily. It softens, loses strength and can crumble at the edges. Paper facing can grow mold if it stays wet. Drywall that was wet only briefly and is dried quickly can sometimes be saved. Drywall that has been soaked or has been wet for days is normally replaced.

Plaster behaves differently. It is harder, and it holds water longer, so it dries slowly. Older plaster over wood lath can hide moisture in the lath and framing behind it. Moisture readings are the only reliable way to tell.

First Steps

1

Stop the water

Close the shutoff for the fixture or the unit. A wall cannot dry while water keeps arriving.

2

Open the area if safe

Remove baseboards, switch plates and outlet covers to let air in. Do not cut into wiring.

3

Cut power to wet circuits

If outlets or switches are wet, turn off that circuit at the panel.

4

Move air and dehumidify

Run fans toward the wall and a dehumidifier in the room.

5

Document

Photograph the wall, the source and any measurements you take.

Pipes, Wiring and Hidden Sources

Many wall leaks start at a pipe fitting, a supply line behind a sink, a shower valve or a drain. Others come from outside, such as a roof leak that runs down the inside of an exterior wall or a window that has failed.

If you cannot find the source, a plumber or a leak detection specialist can use acoustic or thermal tools without opening everything. Fix the source first, then deal with the wall.

Paint, Primer and the Finish

After the wall has dried, repairs follow the usual order: patch, tape, compound, sand, prime and paint. Use a stain-blocking primer if the wall had visible staining. Painting a full wall, from corner to corner, hides patch lines better than painting a small spot.

Exterior Walls Versus Interior Walls

Exterior walls fail for different reasons than interior ones. Failed window flashing, stucco cracks, roof valleys and blocked gutters send water into exterior walls, and the damage often shows low on the wall or around openings.

Interior walls usually fail from plumbing. Look at the walls shared with bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms. A wet exterior wall may need a roofer, a stucco or siding specialist or a window contractor in addition to the drying crew.

Bathroom and Kitchen Walls

These walls are the most likely to hide small plumbing leaks. Supply lines to toilets and sinks, shower valves and dishwasher connections all sit behind the wall surface. A slow drip can soak the cavity for months before a stain shows.

If you see staining near a fixture, shut the water to that fixture and check whether the stain stops growing. A plumber can pressure-test lines to find leaks inside the wall without tearing out more than necessary.

Beverly Hills Utility Work and Wall Leaks

Beverly Hills operates its own water system through the Public Works department. After a street repair or shutoff, water pressure returns and a weak fitting inside a wall can start to drip.

Pressure swings from large utility events, such as the 2018 Sunset Boulevard feeder line break described in local news reports, are a good moment to look at walls around sinks, showers and appliance connections for new dampness.
