When Standing Water Is an Emergency

Water standing in a room is an emergency, whether it came from a burst pipe, an overflowing appliance or a storm. Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait for a crew, shut off the main water valve and lift valuables and electronics off the floor. Keep people and pets away from water that may be contaminated. Cut power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel from dry ground.

Emergency water removal, also called water extraction, is the first physical step. Crews pump or vacuum standing water out and remove as much moisture from carpet and floors as they can. It comes before drying and cleanup, which are covered under water mitigation and water damage cleanup.

Safety first when water meets electricity

  • ✓Never stand in water to reach a panel, outlet or appliance
  • ✓If water is near the panel or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility first
  • ✓Do not use household vacuums on standing water
  • ✓Stay out of any water that came from a sewer or toilet

Burst Pipe Water Damage

A burst pipe is the most common reason for an emergency water call. A supply line under a sink fails, a toilet connection splits or a pipe inside a wall cracks, and water spreads across the floor and into the cavity below. In multistory homes and condos, the water also drops through the floor to the unit beneath.

The first move is the main shutoff valve, so find it before you need it. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, photograph the damage, and call. Burst pipes in older plumbing are often a sign that other sections are wearing out, so ask the plumber who repairs the break to inspect the nearby pipe.

Crews extract the water, check the wall cavity and flooring with moisture meters, and begin drying. A pipe that failed inside a wall can leave more damage than you can see, which is why a meter reading matters more than the visible puddle.

Water in a Garage, Crawl Space or Lower Level

Not every emergency starts inside the living space. A broken irrigation line can flood a crawl space, a failed pump can fill a lower level, and a garage drain that backs up can put inches of water on the slab. If your home has a crawl space, water there can soak insulation and wood framing for days before anyone sees it.

In neighboring West Hollywood, a trunk-line failure in 2026 sent about 17 million gallons down Sunset Boulevard and into underground garages, according to local news reports. Below-grade space fills first and drains last, so extraction there should start as early as possible.
